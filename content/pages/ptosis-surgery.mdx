  - question: "What happens during the recuperation period following surgery?"
    answer: "After a surgical ptosis procedure, patients can go home after a few hours. Pain medication is not needed, and you'll need to wear an eye patch for around 24 hours to reduce swelling. Antibiotic eye ointment and lubricant eye drops will be prescribed for several weeks after the operation. Follow-up appointments will be scheduled, and you should keep the wound clean and dry. Contact your doctor immediately if there are any signs of infection. Avoid strenuous physical activity for 14 days, swimming, wearing contact lenses, and make-up until the wound has completely healed."

  - question: "What are the risks and complications of Ptosis Surgery?"
    answer: "Every surgical treatment has some risk. However, you can be sure you are in competent and safe hands with us. We will go over any potential issues and side effects during your appointment with us. Following your ptosis surgery, you should anticipate a few common symptoms, which often go away in a few days. Some of them include:<br/><br/><strong>Infection:</strong> This is rare; stitches may become infected or inflamed. This can be swiftly settled with oral and topical antibiotics.<br/><br/><strong>Scarring:</strong> Generally, scars are hidden in the skin fold. Stitches are normally visible for the first week, and when they are removed, a faint scar is visible. While the scar may seem thickened and red for 6 to 12 weeks, it will gradually become almost invisible after that period. While not every scar heals equally well, silicone scar remodelling gel can improve the appearance of a thickened or reddened scar. However, the treatment needs to be continued for several months to have a good result.<br/><br/><strong>Large bruise or haematoma:</strong> This can be minimised by using ice packs, avoiding heavy exercise for 2 weeks after surgery, and stopping aspirin and other anticoagulants 2 weeks pre-operatively. If you do experience a haematoma, you may have to go back to have the blood clot removed and your stitches redone.<br/><br/><strong>Asymmetry of eyelid shape, height, upper lid fold, or fullness:</strong> This is rare but can occur. Often, asymmetry may be due to lid bruising and settles as the swelling resolves. Rarely, this may require revision surgery. Occasionally, pre-existing asymmetry of the face, eyebrow, or eyeball position may be responsible; however, your surgeon will discuss this with you prior to the operation.<br/><br/><strong>Blood-stained tears:</strong> This can occur within the first 48-72 hours after surgery and should be managed by gentle, firm, and constant pressure on the shut eyelid for 10-15 minutes.<br/><br/><strong>Recurrence of ptosis:</strong> This can occur early or late after surgery due to suture dissolution, stretching, or scar weakening. It will need to be corrected by reoperation.<br/><br/><strong>Contralateral ptosis:</strong> The position of one droopy eyelid can make the other appear normal. However, immediately after surgery, the &quot;normal&quot; eyelid can droop.       - "Ptosis Surgery, also known as \"Droopy Eyelid Repair,\" is a surgical procedure designed to correct a droopy eyelid and restore balance to the anatomy. It involves adjusting the tendons or the tiny muscles that lift the eyelid. The procedure is often performed with a local anaesthetic injected into the upper lid."
      - "Adult patients receiving local anaesthesia while awake may feel a pulling sensation. Compared to adults, children are more likely to need full general anaesthesia. The surgeon usually needs to make minor levator muscle adjustments. When the eyelid is open, an incision is made in the upper lid, usually in the skin fold that naturally forms there, to hide the stitches and ensuing scars."
