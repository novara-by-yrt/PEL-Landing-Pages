  - question: "Am I suitable for muscle-relaxing injections?"
    answer: "This treatment is not suitable if you are taking the following medications: antibiotics like gentamicin, tobramycin, clindamycin and lincomycin, or quinidine for heart rhythm problems. You should not have muscle-relaxing injections if you have a muscle-weakening disorder (myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton syndrome, ALS/motor neuropathy), are pregnant, breastfeeding, or allergic to human albumin (not egg allergy). Avoid having alcohol in your system, as it can increase the likelihood of bruising and make the injections spread to muscles you don't want to treat."

<h2>Treatment Summary</h2>
<p>Dr Sabrina and her team specialise in managing muscle overactivity that contributes to facial tension, eyelid strain, and dynamic skin creasing. They use chemical neuromodulation (muscle-relaxing injections) to reduce excessive muscle contraction in areas such as the brow, forehead, and around the eyes. This approach helps relieve discomfort, prevent skin irritation, and improve ocular surface comfort while maintaining natural expression.</p>
<p>Her team is among the few specialists offering the refined microdroplet injection technique, which allows precise targeting of superficial muscle fibres for subtle, functional improvement. The focus is on restoring muscle balance, not altering expression.</p>
