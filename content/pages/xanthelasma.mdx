<span>Xanthelasma is a small, yellowish patch that appears under the skin of the eyelid. It's sometimes called a cholesterol deposit. These patches are often seen in people with hyperlipidemia, familial hypercholesterolemia, or primary biliary cirrhosis but can also be seen in menopausal, diabetic, and normo-cholesterolemic patients. However, they can also appear in people with normal cholesterol levels. Women are more likely to get xanthelasma eyelid than men, especially between the ages of 30 and 50. The patches are usually flat, painless, and don't affect vision. But some people may find them unsightly and bothersome.</span><span>You can inherit xanthelasma from your parents, which increases your risk of developing high cholesterol and these yellowish patches.</span><span>Uncontrolled diabetes can lead to high cholesterol and triglyceride levels, increasing the risk of Xanthelasma.</span><span>Excess weight, particularly around the midsection, can contribute to high cholesterol and insulin resistance, leading to Xanthelasma.</span><span>An underactive thyroid gland (hypothyroidism) can cause high cholesterol levels, increasing the risk of Xanthelasma.</span><span>Ongoing inflammation in the body can contribute to high cholesterol and the development of Xanthelasma.</span><span>Drinking too much alcohol can lead to high triglyceride levels, liver disease, and an increased risk of Xanthelasma.</span><span>A diet lacking fibre can contribute to high cholesterol levels, increasing the risk of Xanthelasma.</span><span>Smoking can damage blood vessels, increase inflammation, and contribute to high cholesterol levels, all of which can increase the risk of Xanthelasma.</span>
<h4>Argon Lasers</h4>
<span>An argon laser is a good and easy way to treat small xanthelasma lesions that are less than 1 cm. The laser treats the skin's surface without scarring.</span>
<h4>Trichloroacetic Acid Peels</h4>
<span>Trichloroacetic acid peels can safely treat xanthelasma by removing the top skin layers, reducing the appearance of the lesions and potentially lightening them.</span>
