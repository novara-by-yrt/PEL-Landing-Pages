<span>Most people don't really know their eye shape, and that's okay. But knowing it can actually make a big difference. It helps you understand your face better, and it makes things like makeup, glasses &amp; even eyebrow shapes easier to figure out. Different eye shapes can affect how your eyes appear. They can look more open, more lifted, softer, sharper, and so on.</span>

<span>This guide will help you learn about all the main eye shapes, how to find your own &amp; what makes each one unique.</span>
<h2>Why Eye Shape Matters?</h2>
<span>Eye shape matters because it affects more than just how your eyes look. It plays an important role in makeup, vision, and overall facial symmetry.</span>
<ul><li>
<h3>Makeup</h3>
</li>
</ul>
<span>Different eye shapes need different techniques. What works for almond eyes might not suit hooded or downturned ones. Knowing your shape helps you apply eyeshadow, eyeliner, and lashes in a way that flatters your natural features.</span>
<ul><li>
<h3>Vision</h3>
</li>
</ul>
<span>The shape and length of your eye can influence vision problems like nearsightedness or farsightedness. Even the curve of your cornea matters. Understanding your eye structure can help you stay on top of your eye health.</span>
<ul><li>
<h3>Appearance</h3>
</li>
</ul>
<span>Eye shape contributes to facial symmetry and uniqueness. Recognising yours helps you highlight what makes your face stand out in all the right ways.</span>
<h2>Types of Eye Shapes</h2>
<span>Recognising your eye shape is the initial step in selecting appropriate makeup methods and eyewear designs while also highlighting your natural features.</span>
<h3><strong>1. Almond Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Almond eyes are named because they look like an actual almond. They are slightly pointed at the ends and have a wide centre. The iris touches both the upper and <a href="/surgical/cosmetic-eyelid-surgery/eye-bag-surgery-lower-eyelid-surgery-uk"><strong>lower eyelids</strong></a>, and the outer corners are naturally lifted.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Symmetrical and balanced shape.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Outer corners often appear slightly upturned.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Very versatile for makeup styles.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Almost every makeup technique works well with almond eyes. Winged eyeliner enhances their natural shape, and eyeshadow blends beautifully across the lid without needing heavy adjustments.</span>
<h3><strong>2. Round Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Round eyes appear larger and more open. You’ll often see white space above or below the iris when looking straight ahead.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Iris does not fully touch the eyelids.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Gives a more youthful or surprised expression.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Outer corners may curve slightly upward or downward.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Use eyeliner to elongate the eyes, especially at the outer corners. Avoid heavy liner all around, which can exaggerate the roundness. Smudging eyeshadow outward helps create a more balanced shape.</span>
<h3><strong>3. Monolid Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Monolid eyes have little to no visible crease on the upper eyelid. The surface between the lash line and the brow appears smooth and flat.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>No defined eyelid crease.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>The eyelid may cover most of the upper lash line.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Common among East Asian individuals.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Applying darker shades at the lash line and blending lighter ones toward the brow adds dimension. Tightlining the lashes and using a curled lash helps open up the eyes without relying on a crease.</span>
<h3><strong>4. Hooded Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Hooded eyes have extra skin that droops from the brow bone, covering part or all of the eyelid crease. When the eyes are open, the lid may seem hidden.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>A fold of skin covers the upper crease.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Eyelid appears smaller or less visible.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>May create a tired or droopy look without makeup.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Use deeper matte eyeshadows just above the natural crease to create an illusion of lifted eyelids. Use a waterproof liner and mascara to prevent smudging, as the lash area may touch the skin above.</span>
<h3><strong>5. Upturned Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Upturned eyes naturally tilt upward at the outer corners, creating a soft “cat-eye” appearance.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Outer corners sit higher than inner corners.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Lower lash line has a noticeable upward angle.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Shape naturally appears lifted.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Enhance your natural lift with winged eyeliner or an outward flick of eyeshadow. Be careful not to apply too much, as too much lift can make you look unbalanced.</span>
<h3><strong>6. Downturned Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Downturned eyes slope gently downward at the outer corners. This can give the eyes a soft, sometimes sad or sleepy appearance.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Outer corners are lower than the inner corners.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>The top lid may appear fuller near the outer edge.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Gives a calm, gentle expression.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Use winged eyeliner or lifted eyeshadow to visually raise the outer corners. Keep darker colours on the outer half of the lid and highlight the inner corners for brightness.</span>
<h3><strong>7. Deep-Set Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Deep-set eyes sit further back in the skull. This enhances the prominence of the brow bone while giving a subtle shadow to the eyes.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Eyes appear deeper in the socket.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Brow bone is more pronounced.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Crease is very visible.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Use light or shimmery shades on the lid to bring the eyes forward. Avoid very dark shadows in the crease, which can make the eyes look even more sunken. Focus on curling lashes and opening the eye area.</span>
<h3><strong>8. Protruding Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Protruding eyes bulge slightly outward from the eye socket, making them appear large and rounded.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Eyelid area is very visible.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>The eyes are positioned toward the front of the face.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Can look dramatic or doll-like.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Use darker matte shadows to create depth and balance. Blend shadows upward and outward, and avoid heavy shimmer on the centre lid, which can emphasise the forward-set look.</span>
<h3><strong>9. Close-Set Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Close-set eyes have less space between them than the width of one eye. The inner corners of the eyes are closer together on the face.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Eyes are spaced narrowly apart.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Often creates a more intense or focused expression.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Nose bridge may appear more prominent.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Highlight the inner corners and use eyeshadow to draw attention to the outer corners. Avoid bringing eyeliner too far inward. Winged liner or extending shadow outwards helps create the illusion of wider spacing.</span>
<h3><strong>10. Wide-Set Eyes</strong></h3>
<b>What They Look Like:</b>

<span>Eyes that are wide-set feature a greater distance between them than the width of a single eye. The inner corners are positioned farther away from the nose bridge.</span>

<br /><br />
<b>Key Features:</b>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Eyes appear farther apart.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Creates a more open, relaxed look.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Often seen with broader facial features.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Makeup Tips:</b>

<span>Keep darker shadows and eyeliner focused toward the inner and middle parts of the eye. Avoid overextending the shadow outward. Contouring the sides of the nose can also bring visual balance to wide-set spacing.</span>
<h2>How to Identify Your Eye Shape (Step-by-Step)?</h2>
<span>To know your eye shape, look straight into a mirror under good lighting and note:</span>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>If the crease of your eyelid is not visible or mostly hidden, you likely have hooded or monolid eyes.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>If the outer corners of your eyes tilt upward, your eyes are upturned. If they tilt downward, your eyes are downturned.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>When measuring from the inner corner (tear duct) to the outer corner, if the distance between your eyes is less than the width of one eye, they are considered close-set. If it’s more, they are wide-set.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>If your eyes are located further back within the brow bone, they are considered deep-set. If they seem to extend forward from the sockets, they are more prominent or protruding.</span></li>
</ul>
<span>This simple process helps you figure out which eye shape you have. Once you know that, you can start looking into makeup and grooming tips that are made for your specific eye shape. </span>

<span>Knowing your eye shape helps in choosing flattering makeup or considering cosmetic options.</span>
<h2>Can You Have More Than One Eye Shape?</h2>
<span>Yes, it’s very common to have features from more than one eye shape. Most people don’t fit perfectly into just one category. For example, you might have almond-shaped eyes that are also slightly hooded. Or you could have round eyes that tilt upward at the outer corners, making them a bit upturned too.</span>

<span>Eye shape is not just based on one feature. It’s a mix of several things, like the eyelid crease, the angle of the outer corners, how deep your eyes sit, and the space between them. That’s why many people relate to two or even three types at once.</span>

<span>Understanding this helps you use makeup in a way that works with your unique eye structure.</span>
<h2>Final Thought</h2>
<span>Knowing your eye shape helps you choose what works best for your features. Whether you’re looking for the right eyeliner, glasses, or brow style, understanding your eye shape gives you an advantage.</span>

<span>The best way to figure this out is to stand in front of a mirror and look closely at your features. Try out different looks and adjust as needed. Don’t aim for perfection, focus on what feels right for your face.</span>
