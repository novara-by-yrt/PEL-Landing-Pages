<span>Droopy eyelids are also known as skin or eyelid ptosis. It can be bothersome and make you look older. Things like genetics, getting older, and feeling tired can all cause your eyelids to droop. </span>

<span>This typically happens as we age because our skin and eyelid muscles lose strength over time, causing our eyelids to sag or look hooded. Some people have one droopy eyelid, while others have two.</span>

<span>Unfortunately, loose skin on the eyelids can cause eyesight problems. This is particularly noticeable when the lashes are covered by the upper eyelid. Choosing non-surgical treatments for drooping eyelids may be helpful if you have vision problems that interfere with your daily activities. </span>

<span>Let’s check these non-surgical treatments below in detail:</span>
<h3><strong>Superior Sulcus Fillers</strong></h3>
<span>Superior sulcus fillers can make hollow droopy eyelids look better by adding volume. The results usually last from 6 months to a few years, and the procedure is usually done with a numbing cream. It typically takes about 15 minutes.</span>

<span>The fillers are usually made of hyaluronic acid, which binds to water in the skin to make the tissue fuller. They are a type of injection that is quick, easy, and not very painful. You can get it done in just one visit.</span>
<h3><strong>Dermal Fillers</strong></h3>
Dermal fillers are another popular non-surgical treatment that can help lift droopy eyelids. One type of filler, called hyaluronic acid, adds volume to the skin and boosts the body's natural collagen production. This means it can help restore the skin's structure and fullness over time.
When hyaluronic acid is injected around the eyes, it can replace lost volume and even help with some vision issues. It's especially loved for its long-lasting effects since the increase in collagen can help maintain a youthful appearance longer than many other fillers.
<h3><strong>Sofwave Ultrasound</strong></h3>
<span>Sofwave is an FDA-approved skin tightening treatment. It is a treatment that helps with mild to moderate skin concerns like drooping and sagging. It works by using a special technology called Synchronous Ultrasound Parallel Beam (SUPERB™) to send ultrasound energy deep into the skin, heating it up and making it produce more collagen. This makes the skin look better and reduces droopiness around the eyes.</span>

<span>The treatment can also lift the eyebrows and reduce wrinkles in the periorbital area without surgery. It's a safe and non-invasive way to improve the appearance of the skin.</span>
<h3><strong>Eye Drops</strong></h3>
<span>Eye drops are an alternative for treating drooping eyes if you are not looking for surgery. Upneeq is a prescriptive eye drop and the first medicine licensed by the FDA to treat droopy eyelids. </span>

<span>It has oxymetazoline as an active component. With the help of these eye drops, the muscle that raises the eyelid is contracted, causing it to open more than it could have before treatment.</span>
<h3><strong>Home Remedies</strong></h3>
<span>Home remedies are a natural and cost-effective way to address droopy eyelids. One popular remedy involves applying cucumber slices or cold compresses to the eyes to reduce puffiness and relax the eyelid muscles. You can also try using chamomile tea bags, which have anti-inflammatory properties that can help to soothe &amp; calm the skin.</span>

<span>Another home remedy involves using facial massage techniques to stimulate blood flow and tighten the skin around the eyes. Gently massage your eyelids with a circular motion, using your ring fingers to apply gentle pressure. This can help to increase blood flow &amp; reduce the appearance of droopy eyelids.</span>

<h2><strong>Droopy Eyelid Exercises</strong></h2>
Eyelid exercises can help make droopy eyelids stronger by working the muscles around your eyes.

Here are some exercises you can try:
<ol><li aria-level="1"><span>Eyes warm up</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Basic muscle stimulation</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Eye squeezes</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Eyebrow raises</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Eye blinking exercise</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Eyelid resistance exercise</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Trataka-yogic exercise</span></li>
</ol>
<span>Let’s understand these exercises in detail.</span>
<h3><strong>1. Eye Warm-Up</strong></h3>
Start by cleansing, warming, and gently massaging your eyelids. Warming up the eye muscles enhances flexibility and circulation.
<ul><li>Wash your face and gently cleanse your eyelids.</li>
 	<li>Warm your eyelids with a soft cloth or your palms.</li>
 	<li>Massage your eyelids in circular motions for a few seconds.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>2. Basic Muscle Stimulation</strong></h3>
Stimulating the muscles can help reduce droopiness and improve circulation.
<ul><li>Gently massage your eyelids using your fingertips.</li>
 	<li>Use an electric toothbrush on the lowest setting near your eyelids (avoid direct contact).</li>
 	<li>Do this for a few minutes daily.</li>
</ul>
Spend a few minutes each day stimulating your eyelids, and feel free to mix methods.
<h3><strong>3. Eye Squeezes</strong></h3>
Eye squeezes improve circulation around the eyes and tone the muscles responsible for eyelid elevation.
<ul><li>Sit down with your back straight.</li>
 	<li>Close your eyes tightly and hold for about five seconds.</li>
 	<li>Feel the muscles around your eyes contract.</li>
 	<li>Open your eyes as wide as possible and hold for another five seconds.</li>
 	<li>Repeat this 10-20 times.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>4. Eyebrow Raises</strong></h3>
An eyebrow-raising exercise helps to strengthen the muscles around the eyes and forehead, improving eyelid control.
<ul><li>Sit or stand in a relaxed position.</li>
 	<li>Place your fingers just above your eyebrows.</li>
 	<li>Gently push your eyebrows down while trying to lift them up.</li>
 	<li>Hold the position for a few seconds before releasing.</li>
 	<li>Repeat 10-15 times.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>5. Eye Blinking Exercise</strong></h3>
Blinking exercises enhance muscle coordination and eye strength.
<ul><li>Sit comfortably and relax.</li>
 	<li>Blink rapidly for 10-15 seconds.</li>
 	<li>Close your eyes tightly for five seconds, then open them wide.</li>
 	<li>Repeat the sequence 10-15 times.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>6. Eyelid Resistance Exercise</strong></h3>
Resistance training strengthens eyelid muscles for better control.
<ul><li>Lift your eyebrows and place a finger underneath them for support.</li>
 	<li>Try closing your eyes while keeping your eyebrows raised.</li>
 	<li>Hold the position for a few seconds.</li>
 	<li>Repeat the exercise 10-15 times.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>7. Trataka-Yogic Exercises</strong></h3>
Trataka is a set of yoga practices that are good for eye health. This exercise has four steps:
<ul><li>Sit comfortably with your back straight, either on the floor or a sturdy chair, and keep your palms facing down.</li>
 	<li>With your eyes open, look straight ahead at one point.</li>
 	<li>Without moving your head, slowly turn your eyes to your right shoulder and hold your gaze there for a few seconds without blinking.</li>
 	<li>Slowly return your eye to the centre and repeat the same on the left side.</li>
</ul>
You can also try different movements while keeping your head still. Look up and down, focus on your nose, look up as if trying to see between your eyebrows, and slowly rotate your eyes both clockwise and anticlockwise.

Eyelid exercises could help reduce drooping. This can be accomplished by lifting your eyebrows, putting your finger underneath, and holding them there for a short period of time while attempting to close your eyes. This is like lifting weights for your eyelids. Quick blinking and eye-rolling also help tone your eyelid muscles.

But remember, you'll need to be patient and give in some time - maybe 20 to 30 minutes a day, six or seven days a week - to see any changes.
