<h2>What are Monolids?</h2>
<span>A monolid is an eyelid without a visible crease between the eyelashes and the eyebrows. While monolids are most common among individuals of East Asian descent, they can be found in people of any race or ethnicity, especially those with almond-shaped or elongated eyes.</span>

<span>It’s important to remember that not everyone of Asian descent has monolids. In fact, it’s estimated that around 50% of Asian individuals have this type of eyelid.</span>

<span>Monolids are sometimes mistaken for hooded eyes because they can look somewhat similar. However, hooded eyes have a crease that is hidden under a fold of skin, which creates a “hooded” appearance.</span>

<span>Monolids also shouldn’t be confused with ptosis, a condition where the upper eyelid droops over time due to weakened muscles. Ptosis can develop from ageing, injuries, illnesses, or neurological conditions.</span>

<h2>What are Double Eyelids?</h2>
<span>Double eyelids are when your eyelids have a crease, making it look like there are two eyelids. Many different types of people have double eyelids, including those who are Caucasian, Middle Eastern, African, and Asian. It's more common to have double eyelids than to have monolids because it's a dominant gene.</span>

<span>Some double eyelids have a very clear crease, especially in people with deep-set eyes. Other double eyelids might only have a small crease that's not very noticeable. Double eyelids are different from monolids because they have a curved crease between your eyelids and your eyelashes. As people get older, the skin on their eyelids might start to sag, which can make their eyes look hooded. This can also cause ptosis, where the upper eyelid starts to droop. If it's severe, it can affect your vision, but there are surgeries to fix it.</span>

<h2>Causes of Double Eyelids</h2>
<span>Most people have double eyelids because of their genes. Other factors that may contribute to the presence of double eyelids or the appearance of double eyelids include:</span>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Getting older</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Ptosis</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Accumulation of fat</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Underdevelopment or weakening of the muscles in the eyelids</span></li>
</ul>
<span>Double eyelids may become more noticeable as a person ages. As the skin around the eyes loses its elasticity and becomes looser, the double eyelid may eventually develop into a hooded eye.</span>

<span>Double eyelids are a natural occurrence and generally do not pose any health problems for individuals. However, when the upper eyelids droop or sag, they can start to impact vision.</span>

<span>Drooping or sagging double eyelids can sometimes result in:</span>

<span>The issues mentioned above are more prevalent in older individuals with double eyelids, although the same problems can occur due to specific cosmetic procedures and diseases.</span>
<h2>Asian Eyes vs Caucasian Eyes</h2>
<span>The eyes of Asian and Caucasian people have several differences. For example, Asian eyes usually have a smaller space between the upper and lower eyelids, which is called the palpebral fissure. </span>

<span>Many Asian individuals also have epicanthal folds, which are skin folds that go from the upper eyelid to the inner corner of the eye. When Asians have a double eyelid, the crease is typically lower on the eyelid compared to Caucasian eyes. Additionally, Asian eyelids are often thicker near the brow line and contain more subcutaneous fat compared to Caucasian eyelids.</span>

<span>When Asian individuals undergo surgery to change a monolid (single eyelid) into a double eyelid, the goal is not to make their eyes look Caucasian. Instead, the aim is to create a double eyelid that enhances visibility and expression while still looking natural on an Asian face. </span>

<span>The goal is to make the eyes more expressive without changing their fundamental appearance. This approach respects the natural features of Asian faces while enhancing the eyes' appearance.</span>
