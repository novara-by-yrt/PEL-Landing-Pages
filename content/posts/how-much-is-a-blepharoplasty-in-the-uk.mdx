<h2>Typical Eyelid Surgery Prices and Process</h2>
<span>Eyelid surgery removes excess skin, loose muscle, and bulging fat from the eye area. It helps to restore a rested appearance. It can also help to improve visual function if heavy skin blocks the eyes. As it treats a number of concerns, the cost for eyelid surgery varies widely. The exact price depends on the specific type of surgery required. Here is a breakdown of average UK prices:</span>
<h3>Upper Eyelid Lift Cost</h3>
<span>An </span>upper blepharoplasty cost<span> generally ranges from £5,000 to £6,000 in the UK, with non-surgical options at just £1,000. This procedure treats redundant, hooded skin that rests heavily on the eyelashes, creating visual obstruction.</span>

<span>Surgeons often perform incisions in the natural folds of the eyes,  under a local or twilight anaesthetic. The patient remains awake but feels no pain. The </span>upper eyelid lift cost<span> is slightly less than other procedures.</span>
<h3>Lower Blepharoplasty Cost</h3>
<span>The</span> <span>lower blepharoplasty cost</span><b> usually ranges from £6000 to £11,000. </b>Patients often ask why the <span>blepharoplasty lower cost</span> <span>is much higher. The lower eyelid anatomy is highly complex. It requires immense medical precision. The surgeon must carefully address prolapsed fat pads, skin laxity, and tear trough hollows.</span>

<span>Furthermore, a lower eyelid lift cost usually includes general anaesthetic or twilight sedation. Hospital and overnight stay charges may also apply. These add to the final price.</span>
<h3>Combined Upper and Lower Eyelid Surgery</h3>
<span>Treating both eyelids together is a common choice for facial balance. The price for a combined surgery typically ranges from £6,500 to £11,000 or more.</span>

<span>Combining the procedures is often more cost-effective than two separate surgeries. It combines the surgeon fees, theatre fees, and anaesthesia costs. It also requires only one recovery period for the patient.</span>

<h2>Is Blepharoplasty Available on the NHS?</h2>
<span>Patients frequently search for NHS funding options. The NHS does not cover eyelid surgery for cosmetic reasons, but it does help if your eyelid is causing severe issues.</span>

<span>To qualify for NHS funding, you must prove a strict medical necessity. The criteria are very specific:</span>
<ul><li aria-level="1"><span>Severe eyelid drooping (ptosis) that significantly blocks the visual field.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Visual field tests from an optician that prove a major obstruction.</span></li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><span>Chronic eye infections or severe physical discomfort caused by excess skin.</span></li>
</ul>
<span>Even if a patient meets these strict medical criteria, wait times for non-urgent NHS procedures are often very long. For this reason, many patients seek private care to resolve their issues quickly.</span>
